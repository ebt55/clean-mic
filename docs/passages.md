# Reading passage for the double-talk check

Written for this project (no copyright issues). Read it at a normal speaking
pace, the way you would talk in a meeting. Small slips are fine: the score
compares the raw mic and the cleaned output of the same reading, and the
control read sets your own baseline.

Scoring uses the text between the two markers, lower-cased, punctuation
removed.

<!-- passage:start -->
On Monday morning the office was quiet, so I made a pot of coffee and opened
the window. A light rain was falling on the street below, and a bus stopped at
the corner to let two children climb aboard. I sat down at my desk and read
through the notes from last week. Most of the work was finished, but three
small problems were still waiting for an answer.

The first problem was the printer on the second floor. It had stopped working
on Friday afternoon, and nobody had called for help. I checked the cable and
the paper tray, then turned it off and on again. After a minute it printed a
clean test page, and I wrote a short message to let everyone know.

The second problem was the budget for the new project. The numbers did not add
up, because one invoice had been entered twice. I found the mistake by
comparing the list with the bank statement, line by line. It took almost an
hour, but in the end the total matched to the last cent.

The third problem was harder. Our team had planned a meeting for Thursday, but
half of the people would be travelling that day. We could move it to
Wednesday, or we could hold it online and record it for anyone who missed it. I
asked each person which option they preferred, and most of them chose the
online meeting.

By lunchtime the rain had stopped and the sun was shining on the wet roofs. I
closed the window, packed my notebook into my bag, and walked to the small cafe
across the road. The owner smiled and asked whether I wanted the usual soup
and bread. I said yes, sat by the window, and watched the people hurry past
on their way back to work.
